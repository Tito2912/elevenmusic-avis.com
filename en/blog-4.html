ElevenMusic commercial rights: everything you need for YouTube, ads and streaming in 2026

Published May 9, 2026 • Read time: 10 min

Are you creating monetized YouTube videos, running ads, or planning to distribute your music on Spotify? Before using music generated with ElevenMusic, you need to know exactly which commercial rights are included in your plan. This guide answers all your questions.

Commercial rights are the most sensitive point for any content creator who monetizes. In 2026, ElevenMusic (by ElevenLabs) is one of the few AI music generators with preventive agreements with rights management organizations, making it the safest option for professional use.

Monetized YouTube: what you can do

If you're in the YouTube Partner Program (YPP), you receive a share of ad revenue from your videos. To use ElevenMusic in this context:

  • Required plan: Creator (~$22/month) or higher
  • Process: Generate your music, download in high quality (WAV or MP3)
  • In YouTube Studio: No need to declare it as "third-party licensed music" — ElevenMusic Creator grants you commercial ownership of the generated file
  • Monetization: Enable normally. If YouTube issues an automatic ContentID claim, dispute it by citing your ElevenMusic Creator plan
Practical tip: Keep your ElevenMusic Creator invoice and generation metadata (date, prompt) in case of a ContentID dispute. This is your proof of rights.

Advertising and sponsored content

With the Creator or Pro plan, you can use your ElevenMusic music in:

  • Video ad campaigns (Facebook Ads, Google Ads, YouTube Ads)
  • Radio and TV commercials
  • Commercial podcast intros and jingles
  • Brand videos and corporate presentations
  • Sponsored social media content (Instagram, TikTok)

ElevenMusic has preventive agreements with Merlin (independent label consortium) and Kobalt (major music publisher), significantly reducing legal risk for creators using music in commercial contexts.

Distribution on Spotify, Apple Music and Deezer

Music generated with ElevenMusic Creator can be distributed on streaming platforms via aggregators:

  • DistroKid — accepts AI music if you own commercial rights
  • TuneCore — accepts under similar conditions
  • CD Baby — same policy

Important: disclose in the metadata that the music is AI-generated and that you own the rights. AI music policies at distributors are evolving — check their terms of service before each release.
